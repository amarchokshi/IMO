How I Think About the Role of an Integration Management Office (IMO)

Created by: Amar Chokshi
IMO’s purpose: Create a repeatable, scalable way to integrate acquisition.

Keys to IMO Success

  • Clear leadership alignment on what the IMO owns vs. what functions own
  • Early engagement, ideally starting at the LOI stage, to assess integration complexity and readiness
  • Lightweight governance that enables prioritization and fast decision-making
  • Executive sponsorship to convene cross-functional “tiger teams” when needed
  • Shared accountability for integration success between IMO, functional leaders, and the acquired company

IMO Pillars

Business-Level Alignment

  • Prioritization criteria: Balance deal urgency, business impact, capacity, and interdependencies
  • Strategic alignment: Ensure each acquisition supports organizations’s growth strategy and operating model
  • Leadership alignment: Clarify what the integration means for Clinical Ops, HR, Finance, IT, etc.
  • Success metrics: Define KPIs upfront (stability, adoption, efficiency, retention)

Deal-Level Execution

  • Governance: Clear reporting structure, escalation paths, and decision rights
  • Roles & responsibilities: Simple RACI across IMO and functions
  • Stakeholder mapping: Identify high-impact and high-risk groups early
  • Operating rhythm: Predictable cadence for updates, decisions, and issue resolution

Process Integration

  • Current-state mapping: Identify critical processes, dependencies, and variation
  • Future-state design: Standardize where it creates value; preserve flexibility where clinically necessary

Technology & Data

  • Systems assessment: Evaluate HR, finance, scheduling, and clinical systems
  • Integration approach: Define what migrates, what integrates, and what sunsets
  • Data readiness: Address quality, ownership, and reporting early

People & Adoption

  • Change and communication: Clear, role-based messaging that explains why and what changes
  • Training: Lean, in-the-flow learning for new processes and systems
  • Knowledge management: Single source of truth for integration materials
  • Recognition: Reinforce integration success through visible acknowledgment, not just metrics
